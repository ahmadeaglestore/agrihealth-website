Powdery Mildew

White to grey cotton-like patches on wheat leaves and stems.

Check with AI What to do

Overview

Powdery mildew is a common fungal disease of wheat that shows up as white, cotton-like patches on leaves, stems and sometimes the heads.

It is most common in thick, lush crops with high humidity. Unlike many other diseases, it does not need water drops on the leaf to infect, so it can spread even in dry but humid weather.

Symptoms

  • White to grey fluffy or powdery patches on the upper side of leaves
  • Patches usually start on the lower leaves and move upwards
  • Yellowing of the leaf tissue around and under the patches
  • Small black dots appearing inside older patches later in the season
  • In severe cases, infection on stems and heads

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Blumeria graminis f. sp. tritici.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Wind-blown spores
  • Cool to moderate temperatures with high humidity
  • Dense, thick crops with poor air flow
  • High nitrogen fertiliser that produces soft, lush growth

What to Do

  • Check whether the disease is limited to lower leaves or moving to the upper leaves
  • Ask your agriculture officer if treatment is needed at your crop stage
  • Avoid adding extra nitrogen while the disease is active

When to call an expert: Contact an expert if mildew reaches the upper leaves or the heads early in the season.

Prevention

  • Choose a resistant variety
  • Use balanced fertiliser and avoid very high nitrogen
  • Avoid very high seeding rates that create a dense canopy
  • Remove volunteer wheat plants

Problems That Look Similar

  • Leaf (brown) rust

    Orange-brown powder that rubs off, instead of white growth.
