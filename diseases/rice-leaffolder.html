Rice Leaffolder

Rice leaves folded lengthwise and tied with silk, with a green caterpillar inside and white scraped streaks.

Overview

The rice leaffolder is the caterpillar of a small moth. The caterpillar folds a rice leaf along its length, ties the edges with silk and feeds inside the fold. It scrapes away the green tissue, leaving long white, see-through streaks, so the leaf makes less food for the plant.

It is a known pest of rice in Pakistan, including basmati, and farmers here often spray against it. A study near Peshawar found the attack from the second week of August to the end of September. Heavy fertiliser, shady and humid parts of the field and grassy weeds favour it.

Symptoms

  • Leaves folded lengthwise into a tube, with the edges tied by white silk threads
  • Long white, see-through streaks on the leaf where the green layer has been eaten
  • A green to yellow-green caterpillar inside the fold, with its droppings
  • Small pale moths with dark bands on the wings fly a short way when the crop is disturbed
  • In heavy attack, many folded white leaves make the field look scorched

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Cnaphalocrocis medinalis (moth; the caterpillar does the damage).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Moths fly into fields at night and lay eggs singly on leaves
  • Heavy fertiliser use helps numbers rise fast
  • Humid, shady parts of the field and the rainy season
  • Grassy weeds in fields and on bunds
  • Early broad-spectrum sprays kill natural enemies and can lead to bigger outbreaks later

What to Do

  • Check the upper leaves every week from tillering, especially in August and September
  • Open some folded leaves to see whether live caterpillars are still inside
  • Avoid sprays in the early crop stages; young plants usually recover from leaf damage
  • Do not add extra nitrogen during an attack
  • If many fresh folds with live caterpillars are found at booting or heading, ask your agriculture officer whether a spray is needed and which insecticide is recommended

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if fresh folded leaves with live caterpillars are common on the upper leaves, especially the flag leaf, at booting or heading.

Prevention

  • Remove grassy weeds from fields and bunds
  • Use balanced fertiliser
  • Avoid very dense planting
  • Plough the field after harvest and do not leave a ratoon (regrowth) crop
  • Protect natural enemies such as spiders and small wasps by avoiding unneeded early sprays

Problems That Look Similar

  • Rice hispa

    Also makes white streaks, but the leaves are not folded and small spiny black beetles sit on them.

  • Rice caseworm

    Leaf tips are cut off and rolled into small tubes that float on the water, with the caterpillar inside.

  • Rice stem borer

    The central shoot dries (dead heart) or the panicle turns white; the leaves are not folded.
