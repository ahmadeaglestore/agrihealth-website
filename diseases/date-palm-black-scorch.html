Black Scorch of Date Palm

Hard black, charcoal-like burnt patches on date palm fronds, flower stalks or bud; the heart may rot or bend.

Overview

Black scorch is a fungal disease of date palm. It makes hard, dark brown to black lesions that look as if the tissue has been burnt and turned to charcoal. It can attack the fronds (leaf scorch), the flower clusters (inflorescence blight), the trunk and heart (heart or trunk rot) and the growing bud (bud rot).

When the fungus reaches the growing bud and heart, the palm can die. Some palms recover but keep a clear bend at the point of infection, which is why the disease is also called 'Medjnoon' or fool's disease. Most research reports come from Gulf and Middle Eastern date-growing countries such as Saudi Arabia, Oman, Qatar, the UAE and Iraq; if you see these signs in Pakistan, get them confirmed by the agriculture department or a date palm research station.

Symptoms

  • Dark brown to black, hard, dry lesions on fronds, frond bases or leaf stalks, as if burnt
  • Flower clusters and spathes turn black and dry (inflorescence blight)
  • Young central fronds turn yellow, wilt and dry
  • Rot in the heart or growing bud; the crown may bend or collapse
  • Palms that recover show a sharp bend in the trunk or crown at the infected point
  • In severe cases the whole palm dries and dies

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Thielaviopsis punctulata (Ceratocystis radicicola) and Thielaviopsis paradoxa (Ceratocystis paradoxa), soil-borne wound fungi.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • The fungi live in the soil and enter the palm through wounds
  • Pruning cuts, offshoot removal and other injuries give the fungus a way in
  • Stressed palms and poor orchard care make the disease worse
  • Infected fronds, leaf bases and flower stalks left in the orchard keep the fungus alive
  • Poor irrigation and unbalanced feeding weaken palms

What to Do

  • Cut off affected fronds, leaf bases and flower clusters, collect them and burn them away from the palms
  • Clean pruning tools between palms
  • Ask your agriculture department which approved fungicide should be sprayed on pruning cuts and the tissue around them
  • Mark the palm and watch the bud and young fronds for spreading rot
  • If the heart or bud is badly rotted, remove and burn the palm as advised by experts

When to call an expert: Contact the agriculture department or a date palm research station when you see black, burnt-looking lesions on the bud, heart or young fronds, so the cause can be confirmed.

Prevention

  • Keep the orchard clean; remove dead fronds, old leaf bases and dry flower stalks
  • Prune carefully and avoid unnecessary wounds
  • Protect large cuts as advised by your agriculture department
  • Keep palms healthy with proper irrigation and balanced feeding
  • Take offshoots only from healthy palms

Problems That Look Similar

  • Red palm weevil

    Holes in the trunk or frond bases with brown ooze and chewed fibres; no black charcoal-like lesions.

  • Sudden decline of date palm

    Outer fronds turn orange-yellow from the midrib and dry inwards, without black burnt lesions.

  • Drought or salt scorch

    Frond tips and leaflets dry brown evenly on many palms; no hard black lesions or bud rot.
