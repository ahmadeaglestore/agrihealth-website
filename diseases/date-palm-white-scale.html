White Scale (Parlatoria) of Date Palm

Tiny white to grey-brown scales crowded on date palm leaflets and fruit; leaflets yellow, fruit spoiled.

Overview

The Parlatoria date scale, often called white scale, is a tiny insect that lives fixed under a hard protective cover and sucks sap from date palms. The female cover is small and roundish, light to dark brown with a pale spot, while male covers are smaller, white and long, often in clusters. Heavy colonies coat leaflets, frond bases and fruit so thickly that the surface looks grey-white and crusted.

It is found in most date-growing areas of the world, including Pakistan. It has three to four generations a year, and all stages can be found throughout the year. Damage is most serious on young palms of about two to eight years. Grown palms rarely die, but leaflets turn yellow, fronds die early and infested fruit cannot be sold.

Symptoms

  • Tiny white, grey or brown scale covers crowded on leaflets, frond bases and stalks
  • A discoloured, yellowish spot under each scale on the leaflet
  • Leaflets turn yellow and dry; older fronds die early
  • Fruit covered with scales, shrivelled, misshapen or small, and not fit for sale
  • Young palms grow slowly and stay weak
  • Attack is often patchy, on one to a few palms in a group, mainly on the older fronds

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Parlatoria blanchardi (armoured scale insect).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Moving infested offshoots and planting material carries the scale to new orchards
  • Tiny newly hatched crawlers move to new leaflets and nearby palms
  • Several overlapping generations each year let numbers build up quickly
  • Overuse of broad-spectrum insecticides kills natural enemies and can lead to outbreaks
  • Not pruning old, heavily infested fronds lets the scale build up

What to Do

  • Check the leaflets of older fronds and the fruit bunches for crusts of tiny scales
  • Prune heavily infested old fronds and destroy them away from the orchard
  • Protect ladybird beetles and other natural enemies that feed on the scale
  • On young palms with heavy attack, ask the agriculture department whether a spray, such as an approved oil-based spray, is needed and how to apply it safely

When to call an expert: Contact the agriculture department or a date palm research station if the scale is spreading on young palms or onto the fruit bunches.

Prevention

  • Inspect offshoots before planting and use only clean, scale-free offshoots
  • Do not bring palms or offshoots from infested orchards
  • Prune old, dry and infested fronds regularly
  • Avoid unnecessary broad-spectrum sprays so ladybirds and other natural enemies survive
  • Check young palms regularly, as they suffer most

Problems That Look Similar

  • Red date scale

    A different scale with pinkish-red covers, found mainly on the leaf bases and under the fibres rather than on open leaflets.

  • Mealybugs

    Soft, white, cotton-like insects that can move and make sticky honeydew; scales are hard and fixed in place.

  • Sudden decline of date palm

    Orange-yellow midribs and fronds drying from the outside in, with no scales on the leaflets.
