Downy Mildew (Crazy Top) of Rice

Stunted rice with short, twisted, yellow-spotted leaves, extra tillers and leafy, deformed or missing panicles.

Overview

Downy mildew, also called crazy top, is caused by Sclerophthora macrospora. This is a water mould (not a true fungus) that infects rice, maize, wheat and many grasses. It attacks young plants when they stand in water: resting spores in the soil release swimming spores, which enter seedlings when the soil stays flooded for a day or two at an early stage. The infection then grows inside the plant, and the signs show up weeks later.

Rice downy mildew is uncommon. It appears in older plant disease records for Pakistan, but we found no recent reports of it causing losses here. Look-alike problems such as bakanae, zinc shortage (khaira) and tungro are much more common. If you think you see it, show plants to your agriculture officer to confirm.

Symptoms

  • Seedlings and young plants stay short, with pale yellow spots or stripes on the leaves
  • Leaves are short, narrow, thick and leathery and may be twisted or rolled
  • Infected plants may form more tillers than normal, giving a bushy look
  • Stems have short, bent sections between the nodes
  • Panicles fail to come out, or come out twisted and leafy ('crazy top'), with few or no grains
  • Affected plants are usually in low, waterlogged parts of the field or nursery

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Sclerophthora macrospora (soil- and water-borne downy mildew).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Resting spores survive in the soil and in crop remains for months
  • Flooding or waterlogging of young seedlings lets swimming spores infect them
  • Low spots, poor drainage and heavy rain after sowing raise the risk
  • Grassy weeds and other grass crops can carry the same water mould
  • Very lush growth from too much nitrogen may make the problem worse

What to Do

  • Take some affected plants to your agriculture officer to confirm the cause, because look-alike problems are more common
  • Pull out and destroy plants with leafy, twisted panicles so they do not leave more spores in the soil
  • Improve drainage in low spots and avoid keeping very young seedlings under deep water
  • Remove grassy weeds from bunds and water channels
  • Spraying is rarely worthwhile for this disease; use a product only if the agriculture department advises it

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if you see plants with leafy, twisted panicles, or many stunted, bushy plants in waterlogged parts of a field, so the cause can be confirmed.

Prevention

  • Raise the nursery on level, well-drained land and avoid flooding very young seedlings
  • Level fields well so water does not stand in low spots for long
  • Keep bunds, channels and field edges free of grassy weeds
  • Rotate with a non-grass crop where an outbreak has occurred
  • Use balanced fertiliser and avoid too much nitrogen

Problems That Look Similar

  • Bakanae (foot rot)

    Infected seedlings grow tall, thin and pale yellow-green, taller than healthy plants, and may die; downy mildew plants stay short.

  • Zinc deficiency (khaira)

    Rusty brown spots on leaves and pale bases of young leaves in patches a few weeks after transplanting; panicles are not leafy.

  • Rice tungro

    Yellow-orange leaves starting from the tips, stunting and fewer tillers in patches; spread by green leafhoppers; panicles are not leafy.
