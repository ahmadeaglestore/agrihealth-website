Downy Mildew of Cucurbits

Yellow angular patches between leaf veins, with grey-purple fuzz underneath, on cucumber, melons and gourds.

Overview

Downy mildew is a fast-spreading leaf disease of cucumber, melons, gourds, pumpkin and other cucurbits. It is caused by a fungus-like water mould. Cucumber is the crop most often affected.

It is favoured by very humid air, rain, dew and wet soil, so it is mostly a problem in rainy, humid spells and later in the season. Leaves die early, so fruits may not mature properly and can taste poor.

Symptoms

  • Small pale green to yellow spots on the upper side of older leaves
  • Spots are angular because leaf veins box them in, giving a patchy, mottled look
  • In moist weather, grey to purplish downy growth under the spots, easiest to see early in the morning
  • Spots join and turn brown; whole leaves dry and fall
  • Plants weaken; fruits stay small, do not mature well and taste poor

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Pseudoperonospora cubensis (a fungus-like water mould).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Spores are carried by air currents and by splashing rain or sprinkler water
  • High humidity, rain, dew, cool nights and wet soil favour infection
  • Overhead watering and leaves that stay wet increase the disease
  • It is more common later in the season and in dense, shaded crops
  • Cucurbit weeds and volunteer plants near the field can carry it

What to Do

  • Look under the leaves early in the morning for grey-purple growth to confirm the disease
  • In small plots, remove and destroy badly affected leaves; don't leave them in the field
  • Stop overhead watering; water the furrows in the morning so leaves dry quickly
  • Contact your agriculture officer quickly; downy mildew moves fast, and they can advise whether a spray is needed and which product is approved

When to call an expert: Call your agriculture officer as soon as you see angular yellow spots with fuzz underneath, especially in rainy or humid weather.

Prevention

  • Grow resistant or tolerant varieties, especially of cucumber
  • Trellis cucumbers and gourds and give enough spacing for air flow
  • Avoid overhead or sprinkler irrigation; use furrow or drip irrigation
  • Where possible, plant early so the crop avoids the most humid period
  • Control cucurbit weeds along field edges and water channels
  • Destroy crop remains after the last harvest

Problems That Look Similar

  • Powdery mildew

    White flour-like powder on top of the leaf that rubs off; spots are not boxed in by veins.

  • Mosaic virus

    Light and dark green mottling with small, crinkled leaves, but no dead brown patches and no fuzz underneath.

  • Nutrient deficiency

    Even yellowing of older leaves or between veins, without sharp angular patches or any growth underneath.
