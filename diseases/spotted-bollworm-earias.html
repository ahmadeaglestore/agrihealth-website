Spotted Bollworm (Okra Shoot and Fruit Borer)

Shoot tips droop and dry; small spotted caterpillars bore into okra pods and cotton squares and bolls.

Overview

Spotted bollworm is the caterpillar of a small moth with green front wings. Two species are involved: Earias vittella (spotted bollworm) and Earias insulana (spiny bollworm). Both attack cotton and okra, and they also live on wild plants of the same (mallow) family.

Before flowering, the caterpillar bores into the tender shoot tip, which droops and dries. Later it bores into squares, flowers, bolls and okra pods. Damaged squares and young bolls drop, bored bolls can rot, and bored okra pods become bent and unfit for sale.

Symptoms

  • The tip of the main shoot droops, wilts and dries in young plants
  • Small holes with droppings in shoot tips, squares and flowers
  • Bracts of damaged squares open outwards (flare), and squares and young bolls drop
  • Bolls with holes that may rot
  • Okra pods with holes, often bent or misshapen, with a caterpillar inside
  • Small brown caterpillars with white lines and spots or small fleshy points on the body

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Earias vittella and Earias insulana (caterpillars of small green moths).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Moths fly into fields from nearby okra, cotton and wild host plants
  • Okra and cotton grown close together let the pest move between crops
  • Wild plants of the mallow family keep it alive between crops
  • Broad-spectrum sprays that kill natural enemies let numbers rise

What to Do

  • Pick off and destroy drooping shoot tips with the caterpillar inside
  • Collect and destroy bored okra pods and fallen squares and bolls; don't leave them in the field
  • Put up pheromone traps to see when moths are active
  • Ask your agriculture officer whether damage is above the local action level and which approved spray, if any, to use

Don't

  • Don't leave bored okra pods or fallen squares lying in the field
  • Don't pick okra for sale soon after a spray; follow the waiting period on the label

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer when many shoot tips are drooping in young cotton or okra, or when many squares, bolls or pods have holes.

Prevention

  • Remove wild mallow-family weeds and other alternate hosts around fields
  • Destroy cotton crop leftovers after harvest
  • Pick okra regularly and destroy damaged pods
  • Protect natural enemies such as Trichogramma egg wasps and lacewings
  • Use pheromone traps every season for early warning

Problems That Look Similar

  • American bollworm

    A bigger, smooth caterpillar that bores large round holes and often feeds half outside the fruit; it does not make shoot tips droop.

  • Pink bollworm

    A pink caterpillar hidden inside bolls and seeds, with twisted 'rosette' flowers; shoot tips are not attacked.

  • Brinjal shoot and fruit borer

    Causes similar drooping shoots and bored fruits, but on brinjal; spotted bollworm attacks okra and cotton.
