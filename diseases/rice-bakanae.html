Bakanae (Foot Rot) of Rice

Some rice seedlings grow unusually tall, thin and pale, then rot at the base and die.

Overview

Bakanae is a Japanese word meaning 'foolish seedling'. The fungus makes a plant growth hormone, so infected seedlings shoot up taller and thinner than healthy ones. Most of these plants die early or give empty panicles. Because the base of the plant rots, the disease is also called foot rot.

It is an important problem in basmati rice, mostly seen in the nursery and in the weeks after transplanting in June and July. The fungus is carried mainly on seed, so clean seed and seed treatment are the most important steps.

Symptoms

  • Seedlings in the nursery that stand clearly taller and thinner than the rest
  • These tall plants are pale yellow-green and have few tillers
  • Roots growing out from the lower nodes of the stem
  • Pinkish-white fungal growth at the base of the stem and on the nodes
  • The base of the plant rots; seedlings or tillers wilt and die
  • Plants that survive give empty or poorly filled panicles

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Fusarium fujikuroi (Gibberella fujikuroi), seed- and soil-borne fungus.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Infected seed is the main source; spores sit on and inside the seed
  • The fungus can also survive in soil and crop residue
  • Spores from dying plants spread with wind and water and contaminate healthy grain at harvest
  • High temperatures (above about 30 °C), wet nurseries and too much nitrogen favour the disease

What to Do

  • Pull out tall, pale plants as soon as you see them, with their roots, and bury them away from the field
  • Check the nursery carefully before transplanting and do not transplant suspicious seedlings
  • Avoid extra nitrogen in affected fields
  • Ask your agriculture officer about approved seed treatment or seedling root-dip for the next nursery

Don't

  • Don't keep seed from an affected field
  • Don't throw pulled plants into water channels or leave them in the field

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if many tall, pale seedlings appear in the nursery, or if plants keep dying in patches after transplanting.

Prevention

  • Use certified, disease-free seed
  • Before sowing, put seed in salt water and throw away the light seeds that float
  • Treat seed with an approved fungicide as advised by your agriculture department
  • Use balanced nitrogen fertiliser
  • Plough infected stubble into the soil soon after harvest instead of burning it, and rotate crops where possible

Problems That Look Similar

  • Zinc deficiency (khaira)

    Plants are short and stunted with rusty-brown spots, not tall and thin.

  • Nitrogen deficiency

    The whole field is evenly pale and short; no unusually tall plants.

  • Stem borer dead heart

    Only the central shoot dries and pulls out easily; the plant is not taller than others.
