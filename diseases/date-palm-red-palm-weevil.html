Red Palm Weevil

Holes in the date palm trunk or frond bases ooze brown liquid and push out chewed fibres; the crown wilts.

Overview

The red palm weevil is one of the most dangerous pests of date palm and coconut. The adult is a large reddish-brown beetle with dark spots and a long snout. The female lays eggs in wounds and soft tissue of the palm, and the legless, creamy-white grubs with a brown head tunnel inside the trunk and crown.

The pest is present in Pakistan, including the date-growing areas of Sindh. Because the grubs stay hidden inside, early attack is hard to see; by the time the crown wilts, the palm is often badly hollowed and can die or break. Palms with cuts or wounds are attacked most.

Symptoms

  • Small holes on the trunk, at frond bases or near offshoots
  • Thick brown, bad-smelling liquid oozing from the holes
  • Chewed fibres pushed out of the holes and collecting at frond bases or the foot of the palm
  • Cocoons made of fibres at frond bases or at the base of the palm
  • Inner fronds wilt, dry and droop, and the crown may look lopsided or umbrella-like
  • In quiet surroundings a gnawing sound may be heard inside the trunk
  • In late stages the crown or trunk can break and fall

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Rhynchophorus ferrugineus (weevil whose grubs tunnel inside the trunk).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Adult weevils fly long distances and are drawn to wounded, damaged or dying palms
  • Females lay eggs in cuts from pruning, offshoot removal, climbing steps and other wounds
  • Moving infested offshoots and palms to new areas carries the pest
  • Dead or neglected infested palms left standing produce new weevils
  • Rotting leaf bases, a high water table and very lush growth increase the risk

What to Do

  • Inspect every palm, especially the trunk base, frond bases and offshoots, for holes, brown ooze and chewed fibres
  • Report a suspected attack quickly to your agriculture department or a date palm research station; treatment works best early
  • Use pheromone traps with a fermenting food bait to catch adult weevils; check and empty them regularly
  • Treatment of an infested palm should be done by or under the guidance of trained agriculture staff
  • Badly infested palms that cannot be saved should be cut down, chopped and burnt so the grubs and pupae inside are killed

Don't

  • Don't move offshoots or palm logs from an infested orchard to new areas
  • Don't leave cut infested palms lying in the orchard without destroying them

When to call an expert: Contact the agriculture department as soon as you see oozing holes or chewed fibres on a palm; quick action can save the palm and protect nearby trees.

Prevention

  • Avoid wounding palms; don't cut steps into the trunk for climbing
  • When removing offshoots or fronds, cut cleanly and protect the cut surface as advised by your agriculture officer
  • Keep the crown and frond bases clean of rotting debris
  • Buy offshoots only from known pest-free sources and check them before planting
  • Keep pheromone traps in the orchard for regular monitoring
  • Don't leave dead or dying palms standing in the orchard
  • Work together with neighbouring growers; area-wide action works best

Problems That Look Similar

  • Sudden decline of date palm

    Fronds turn orange-yellow and dry from the outer leaves inwards, with no holes, ooze or chewed fibres on the trunk.

  • Termites

    Thin mud tunnels or soil sheets on the trunk and base; no brown ooze or large grubs.
