Rice Stripe Virus

Pale yellow broken stripes on rice leaves; young leaves droop and wilt; plants stunted with few panicles.

Overview

Rice stripe virus causes broken pale yellow or white stripes along rice leaves and stunting. In young plants the newest leaf stays rolled, grows long, then droops and curls and may die. The virus is spread only by planthoppers, mainly the small brown planthopper (Laodelphax striatellus). This insect keeps the virus for life and passes it to most of its eggs. It also lives on wheat, maize and many grasses.

This disease is a serious problem in East Asia, mainly Japan, China and Korea. It has not been reported in Pakistan. The small brown planthopper itself was first recorded in Punjab, Pakistan, in 2020, but the virus has not been found here. In Pakistan, striped or yellow rice leaves are far more likely to be caused by zinc shortage (khaira), tungro or bacterial leaf streak. If you see symptoms that match this page and the common causes are ruled out, report them to your agriculture department so samples can be tested.

Symptoms

  • Pale yellow to white stripes or broken streaks along the leaves
  • In young plants, the newest leaf stays rolled, grows long and then droops and curls, like a wilting leaf
  • Badly infected seedlings turn grey-brown and die
  • Plants are stunted and form few tillers
  • Few panicles; those that form are small, deformed or partly empty
  • Plants infected later show milder stripes but ripen poorly

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Rice stripe virus (a tenuivirus) spread by the small brown planthopper, Laodelphax striatellus.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Small brown planthoppers pick up the virus from infected plants and spread it while feeding
  • Female planthoppers pass the virus to most of their eggs, so newly hatched young can carry it without feeding on sick plants
  • Planthoppers move from wheat, barley and grasses into young rice fields
  • The virus is not known to spread through seed, soil, water or by touch

What to Do

  • First check for the common look-alikes: zinc shortage (khaira), tungro and bacterial leaf streak
  • If stripes with drooping young leaves keep spreading and the common causes are ruled out, report to your agriculture department and give samples for testing
  • Pull out and destroy badly affected plants while there are only a few
  • Look for planthoppers at the base of the plants by tapping them over a tray or a pan of water
  • Follow the agriculture department's advice on planthopper control; don't spray without need, because sprays kill natural enemies and can make planthoppers worse

When to call an expert: Report to your agriculture department if many young rice plants show pale stripes with drooping new leaves that are not explained by zinc shortage, tungro or bacterial leaf streak, because this virus is not known in Pakistan.

Prevention

  • Use healthy seedlings and grow varieties recommended for your area
  • Keep bunds and channels free of grassy weeds that shelter planthoppers
  • Destroy volunteer rice and stubble after harvest
  • Report unusual stripe symptoms early so new problems can be identified quickly

Problems That Look Similar

  • Zinc deficiency (khaira)

    Rusty brown spots and pale bases of young leaves in patches soon after transplanting; plants improve when zinc is applied.

  • Rice tungro

    Yellow to orange leaves starting from the tips, with stunting in patches; spread by green leafhoppers.

  • Bacterial leaf streak

    Narrow water-soaked streaks between the veins that turn yellow-brown, often with tiny yellow beads of ooze; young leaves do not droop.
