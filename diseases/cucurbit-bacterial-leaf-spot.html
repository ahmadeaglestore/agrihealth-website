Bacterial Leaf Spot of Gourds and Pumpkin

Small water-soaked spots with yellow halos on leaves that turn brown or black; small crusty spots on fruit.

Overview

Bacterial leaf spot, also called Xanthomonas leaf spot, is caused by a bacterium that can attack all cucurbits but is seen most on pumpkin, squash and gourds. It starts as small water-soaked spots on the underside of leaves, which show as yellow spots on the upper side, and then turn brown or black with a yellow border. A similar disease, angular leaf spot, is caused by a different bacterium and spreads in the same way.

The bacteria come in with infected seed or survive on crop remains and some weeds, and are spread by splashing water. Warm, humid, wet weather, rain and overhead watering favour the disease. Leaf spots weaken the plant, and fruit spots let rot organisms in, so fruit can rot and the seed inside can become infected.

Symptoms

  • Small water-soaked spots on the underside of leaves, with matching yellow spots on the upper side
  • Spots turn brown to black with a yellow border (halo)
  • Spots may be round, or angular where the veins stop them; many spots join and twist the leaf
  • Centres of old spots dry and may tear, leaving ragged holes
  • Small, round, slightly sunken spots on fruit with tan or white crusty centres
  • Fruit spots can crack the rind and lead to rot inside the fruit

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Xanthomonas cucurbitae (seed-borne bacterium); the look-alike angular leaf spot is caused by Pseudomonas syringae pv. lachrymans.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Infected seed brings the disease into new fields
  • Bacteria survive on infected crop remains and some weeds for at least two years
  • Rain splash and overhead irrigation spread bacteria from plant to plant
  • Workers and tools brushing against wet, infected leaves
  • Warm, humid weather with frequent rain

What to Do

  • When only a few plants are affected, pull them out and destroy them
  • Stop overhead watering; use furrows or drip, and water when leaves can dry quickly
  • Do not work in the field or pick fruit when the leaves are wet
  • Ask your agriculture officer whether a copper-based spray is advised; sprays only slow the disease, work best before many spots appear, and are of little use once fruit are spotted

Don't

  • Do not save seed from fruit with spots

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if spots spread quickly after rain, or if the fruit begin to show spots.

Prevention

  • Use certified, disease-free or treated seed
  • Rotate out of cucurbits for at least one year, and better for three years
  • Destroy or deep-plough crop remains after harvest
  • Use drip or furrow irrigation instead of sprinklers
  • Control weeds in and around the field
  • Grow resistant cucumber varieties where they are available

Problems That Look Similar

  • Angular leaf spot

    Angular spots held between the veins, with a white crust of dried bacterial ooze under the leaf; old spots fall out and leave holes. Managed the same way.

  • Downy mildew

    Larger yellow angular patches with grey-purple fuzzy growth underneath in the morning, and no spots on the fruit.

  • Anthracnose

    Larger brown spots that crack into holes, and sunken fruit spots with pink ooze in wet weather.
