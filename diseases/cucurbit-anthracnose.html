Anthracnose of Gourds, Melons and Cucumber

Round brown leaf spots that crack into holes, and sunken dark fruit spots with pink ooze in wet weather.

Overview

Anthracnose is a fungal disease of cucurbits. Cucumber, melon (kharbooza) and watermelon (tarbooz) are the most affected; squash and pumpkin are rarely attacked. The fungus can attack all parts above the ground and forms brown to black spots on leaves, stems and fruit.

The disease needs warm, wet weather. It usually builds up in the middle of the season after the vines have covered the ground, and is worst in rainy spells such as the monsoon and in crops watered over the leaves. Spotted fruit cannot be sold, often taste bitter and rot quickly.

Symptoms

  • Small, water-soaked or yellowish spots on leaves, often near the veins
  • Spots grow into round brown patches (cucumber, melon) or dark brown to black irregular patches (watermelon)
  • Centres of old spots crack and fall out, leaving 'shot holes', and leaves look ragged
  • Long, sunken spots on stems and leaf stalks; in melon the vines may wilt
  • Round, sunken, dark spots on fruit, covered with pink or salmon-coloured sticky spore masses in wet weather
  • Spotted fruit may taste bitter and often rot

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Colletotrichum orbiculare (fungus carried on seed and crop remains).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Seed taken from infected fruit
  • Spores splash from infected crop remains, volunteer plants and weeds in rain or overhead irrigation
  • Workers, tools and beetles carry spores when they move through wet plants
  • Warm, humid, rainy weather; spores need a film of water on the leaf to infect

What to Do

  • Remove badly spotted leaves and fruit and take them out of the field
  • Do not work in the crop or pick fruit while the leaves are wet
  • Water in furrows or by drip, not over the leaves
  • Ask your agriculture officer whether a fungicide spray is needed and which product is approved; sprays protect healthy leaves but do not heal spots
  • Handle fruit gently at harvest so it is not wounded

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if spots are spreading quickly in wet weather or the fruit are getting sunken spots.

Prevention

  • Use certified, disease-free seed; do not save seed from spotted fruit
  • Do not grow any cucurbit in the same field for at least one year, and better for two to three years
  • Plough crop remains deep or remove them soon after harvest
  • Remove volunteer cucurbit plants and weeds
  • Grow resistant cucumber and watermelon varieties where they are available

Problems That Look Similar

  • Alternaria leaf blight

    Brown spots with clear target rings that start on older leaves near the centre of the plant, and no pink spore masses on fruit.

  • Bacterial leaf spot

    Many small water-soaked spots with yellow halos, often angular between veins; fruit spots are small and crusty with no pink ooze.

  • Downy mildew

    Yellow, angular patches bounded by veins, with grey-purple fuzzy growth under the leaf in the morning.
