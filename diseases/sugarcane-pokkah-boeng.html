Pokkah Boeng (Top Twist) of Sugarcane

Young top leaves are pale at the base, wrinkled and twisted; in bad cases the top rots.

Overview

Pokkah boeng is a fungal disease of the young top of the sugarcane plant. The name means 'distorted top' in Javanese. Young leaves come out pale, wrinkled, twisted and short. In bad cases the growing point dies (top rot), or the stem shows cuts as if slashed with a knife.

It is favoured by warm, humid weather, especially summer showers with cloudy skies, so in Pakistan it is most likely during the monsoon when the crop is growing fast. The Sindh Agriculture Department lists it among sugarcane diseases. Many plants recover when the weather improves, but top rot can kill canes.

Symptoms

  • Pale or yellow-white patches at the base of the young top leaves
  • Young leaves wrinkled, twisted and shorter than normal, sometimes tangled together
  • Reddish specks or stripes on the pale parts of the leaves
  • Short internodes and buds sprouting early on the cane
  • In bad cases the growing point and top rot and die (top rot)
  • Sometimes cuts across the stem that look as if made with a knife (knife-cut stage)

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Fusarium species (F. sacchari, F. verticillioides – earlier F. moniliforme – and F. proliferatum).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Spores are carried mainly by air currents
  • It also spreads through infected setts, irrigation water, rain splash and soil
  • Warm, humid weather with summer showers and cloudy skies
  • Highly susceptible varieties

What to Do

  • Look at the youngest leaves for pale bases, wrinkling and twisting
  • Pull out and destroy plants with top rot or knife-cut stems
  • Remove dry lower leaves (detrashing) to let air move through the crop
  • Give balanced fertiliser and micronutrients as advised by your agriculture department
  • If many plants show top rot, ask your agriculture officer whether a fungicide spray is needed and which one is recommended

Don't

  • Do not take setts from affected plants for planting

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if many plants show top rot or knife cuts, or if twisted tops keep spreading after the rains. They can confirm the disease and advise on a less susceptible variety.

Prevention

  • Avoid highly susceptible varieties; grow a variety recommended for your area
  • Use setts from healthy fields
  • Remove dry leaves and keep the field clean
  • Give balanced nutrition after each ratoon and at earthing up
  • Remove and destroy top-rot plants early

Problems That Look Similar

  • Top borer damage

    Dead heart with rows of shot holes in the leaves and a red tunnel in the midrib; a caterpillar is found inside.

  • Banded chlorosis (cold injury)

    Pale bands run straight across the leaf after cold weather; leaves are not wrinkled or twisted.

  • Whip smut

    A long black whip comes out of the top; no pale, twisted young leaves.
