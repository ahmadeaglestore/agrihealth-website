Sudden Decline of Date Palm

Date palm fronds turn orange-yellow from the midrib and dry from the outer to inner fronds; trees die in months.

Overview

Sudden decline, also called date palm wilt, is a serious disease of date palm in Sindh, especially in Khairpur, the main date-growing area of the province. Research in Sindh found the soil-borne fungus Fusarium solani as the main fungus in affected trees, together with some other fungi.

Affected palms usually die within three to six months, and sometimes linger for one to two years without bearing fruit. Surveys in Khairpur found the disease at many locations, with the share of affected trees varying widely from place to place. Waterlogged, poorly drained land and stressed trees are hit hardest.

Symptoms

  • Orange-yellow colour on the midrib of a frond, then on its leaflets
  • Symptoms start on the outer, lower fronds and move towards the young central fronds
  • Each frond dries from its tip and turns pale brown
  • Early infection makes fruits drop; later infection dries the bunches at the khalal or rutab stage
  • The tree stops fruiting and the whole crown dries
  • The tree usually dies within months, and neighbouring palms may later show symptoms

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Mainly linked to Fusarium solani (soil-borne fungus); other fungi and stress such as waterlogging also play a part.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • The fungus lives in the soil and attacks the palm from there
  • Waterlogging and poor drainage, especially on low-lying land and near canals, increase the disease
  • Standing water from rice grown under palms, or daily watering of grass around palms, adds to the risk
  • Weak or stressed trees, and trees damaged by termites, are more likely to be affected
  • Moving soil or offshoots from affected orchards may carry the problem to new areas

What to Do

  • Mark affected palms and contact your agriculture department or a date palm research institute to confirm the cause
  • Cut and burn dried, infected fronds; keep the trash out of the orchard
  • Improve drainage and switch to periodic irrigation instead of standing water
  • Ask the agriculture department whether an approved fungicide treatment should be applied to affected and nearby trees, and follow their instructions
  • Remove and burn dead palms so they do not remain a source of infection

Don't

  • Don't flood-irrigate or let water stand around palms
  • Don't take offshoots from affected trees or orchards for new planting

When to call an expert: Contact the agriculture department or a date palm research station as soon as the midribs of outer fronds turn orange-yellow, especially if nearby palms have died.

Prevention

  • Plant on well-drained land; avoid low-lying, waterlogged sites near canals or raise the soil level
  • Use periodic irrigation and avoid growing rice or watering grass under palms
  • Keep palms healthy with balanced feeding as advised by experts
  • Control termites and other pests that weaken trees
  • Take offshoots only from healthy trees in disease-free orchards
  • Keep the orchard clean; burn infected fronds and dead trees

Problems That Look Similar

  • Red palm weevil

    Holes in the trunk or frond bases with brown ooze and chewed fibres; the inner, young fronds wilt first.

  • Termites

    Mud tunnels and soil sheets on the trunk or base; damage starts at the base, not as orange-yellow midribs.

  • Drought or salt stress

    Fronds dry evenly on many palms at once, usually after poor watering or on salty soil, and trees improve with better care.
