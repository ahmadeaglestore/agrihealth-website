Blossom End Rot

A dark, sunken, leathery patch at the bottom (flower end) of tomato, chilli or gourd fruits.

Overview

Blossom end rot is not caused by a germ. It happens when the growing fruit does not get enough calcium. Calcium moves into the plant with water, so when the soil goes from wet to dry, or roots are damaged, the fruit runs short even if the soil has enough calcium.

It usually shows on the first fruits of the season, and is common in hot, dry, windy weather, with irregular irrigation turns, and in salty soils. It cannot spread from plant to plant, and spraying fungicides or insecticides will not help. Even watering is the main fix.

Symptoms

  • A small, water-soaked, bruise-like spot at the bottom (flower end) of young fruit
  • The spot grows, turns dark brown to black, and becomes sunken, flat and leathery
  • It can cover up to half of the fruit
  • The first fruits of the season are usually affected most
  • On chilli the spot may look pale like sunscald; on gourds the tip of the fruit shrivels
  • Moulds or rot often grow on the damaged patch later

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Too little calcium reaching the growing fruit, usually because of uneven watering rather than low soil calcium.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Uneven watering: soil drying out and then being heavily watered
  • Hot, dry, windy weather or drought when fruits are growing fast
  • Too much nitrogen, especially ammonium-type fertiliser, which pushes leaf growth
  • Salty soil or high sodium, potassium or magnesium that block calcium uptake
  • Root damage from hoeing too close to plants, waterlogging or very dry soil
  • It is not infectious and does not spread between plants

What to Do

  • Pick off affected fruits; they will not recover and waste the plant's energy
  • Water regularly so the soil stays evenly moist, not soaked and then dry
  • Mulch around plants with straw or dry grass to keep moisture in
  • Stop giving extra nitrogen fertiliser for now
  • Avoid hoeing close to the plants

Don't

  • Do not spray fungicides or insecticides for this problem; they will not help

When to call an expert: Ask your agriculture officer for a soil and water test if blossom end rot keeps coming back even with regular watering, or if your soil or water may be salty.

Prevention

  • Water evenly through the season; drip irrigation or short, regular irrigation turns help
  • Mulch to keep soil moisture steady in hot weather
  • Get a soil test before planting and correct calcium or pH on your agriculture department's advice
  • Use balanced fertiliser and avoid too much nitrogen
  • Grow varieties that are less prone to blossom end rot where available
  • Avoid damaging roots during weeding and hoeing

Problems That Look Similar

  • Fruit fly damage

    A small puncture mark and soft rot with maggots inside; it can be anywhere on the fruit, not only the bottom.

  • Sunscald

    Pale, papery patch on the side of the fruit facing the sun, not at the bottom.

  • Chilli anthracnose

    Sunken spots with rings of tiny dark dots or orange spore masses, anywhere on the fruit.
