Termites

Plants dry up in patches and pull out easily; roots and stem base eaten, with soil-filled mud tunnels.

Overview

Termites (deemak) are small, pale, soft-bodied insects that live in colonies in the soil. They eat roots, stems and seed, and they cover the parts they feed on with soil, making mud tunnels and crusts. In Pakistan they damage wheat, sugarcane, maize and cotton, and they also attack the roots and trunks of mango, citrus and other fruit trees.

Damage is worst in dry, rain-fed (barani) and un-irrigated areas and when the soil is short of water. In wheat it shows as dry plants scattered or in patches and white, empty ears; in sugarcane the attack is most serious at germination, when the planted setts are eaten and gaps appear.

Symptoms

  • Plants wilt, turn yellow and dry, singly or in patches
  • Dried plants pull out easily because the roots are eaten
  • Roots and the stem base eaten, often filled or covered with soil
  • In wheat, white empty ears late in the season
  • Poor germination of sugarcane setts and wheat seed, leaving gaps in the field
  • Mud tunnels or soil crusts on tree trunks, stems and stubble, with small pale insects inside

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Subterranean termites, e.g. Microtermes obesi and Odontotermes obesus.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Dry soil and lack of irrigation, especially in rain-fed (barani) areas
  • Crop stubble, dead wood and plant leftovers in the field give termites food
  • Raw (undecomposed) farmyard manure attracts termites
  • Termite colonies and mounds in or near the field
  • Damage rises as crops near maturity and under drought stress

What to Do

  • Dig around a few dried plants to confirm termites on the roots
  • Irrigate where possible; moist soil reduces attack
  • Find termite mounds near the field and destroy them, including the queen if you can find her
  • Ask your agriculture officer about an approved soil, seed or sett treatment for your crop
  • On fruit trees, scrape off mud tunnels from the trunk and check the roots; ask your horticulture officer for treatment advice

Don't

  • Don't apply raw, undecomposed farmyard manure
  • Don't leave crop stubble and dead wood lying in or around the field

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer when plants are drying in many patches, when seed or sugarcane setts germinate poorly because of termites, or when fruit trees show mud tunnels and start to decline.

Prevention

  • Use only well-rotted farmyard manure or compost
  • Irrigate on time and avoid long dry spells where water is available
  • Remove stubble, crop leftovers and dead wood from fields and orchards
  • Destroy termite mounds around the field
  • In fields with a history of termites, use the seed or sett treatment recommended by your agriculture department

Problems That Look Similar

  • Cutworms

    Cutworm caterpillars cut young plants at soil level at night; you find a fat grey caterpillar curled in the soil, not small pale insects.

  • Stem borers

    Borers make holes in the stem and a dead central shoot; the roots are not eaten and there are no mud tunnels.

  • Root-knot nematodes

    Plants are stunted and wilt in the heat, but the roots have knots and are not eaten.
