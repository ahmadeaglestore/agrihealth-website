Mosaic and Leaf Curl Viruses of Cucurbits

Cucurbit leaves show yellow-green mosaic, curling or crinkling; plants stunted, fruits small or misshapen.

Overview

Several viruses cause mosaic and leaf curl in cucurbits. Cucumber mosaic virus and potyviruses such as Zucchini yellow mosaic virus are carried by aphids. Tomato leaf curl New Delhi virus, a whitefly-borne virus present in Pakistan, also infects tomato, chilli, brinjal, okra and cotton.

Infected plants cannot be cured. Early infection gives stunted plants with few, small, misshapen fruits, while late infection causes less loss. Control depends on healthy seedlings, removing infected plants early and keeping aphids and whiteflies away from young crops.

Symptoms

  • Veins on young leaves become pale (vein clearing)
  • Light green or yellow and dark green patches (mosaic or mottling) on leaves
  • Leaves are small, crinkled, blistered or narrow
  • With leaf curl virus: leaves curl and roll, with yellow mosaic, swollen veins and short gaps between leaves
  • Plants are stunted and bushy and flower poorly
  • Fruits are small, misshapen, mottled or have rough, warty skin

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Cucumber mosaic virus and potyviruses (e.g. ZYMV, WMV, PRSV) spread by aphids; Tomato leaf curl New Delhi virus (ToLCNDV) spread by whitefly.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Aphids pick up mosaic viruses within minutes of feeding and pass them to the next plant
  • Whiteflies carry leaf curl virus and keep spreading it for a long time after feeding on a sick plant
  • Infected weeds, volunteer cucurbits and nearby old crops act as virus sources
  • Spread is fastest when aphid or whitefly numbers are high
  • Nearby tomato, chilli, okra and cotton crops can share the whitefly-borne virus

What to Do

  • Pull out plants with clear symptoms early in the crop and destroy them away from the field
  • Remove cucurbit weeds and volunteer plants around the field
  • Hang yellow sticky traps to monitor aphids and whiteflies
  • Ask your agriculture officer for advice on controlling whitefly or aphids; sprays alone rarely stop virus spread

Don't

  • Don't keep infected plants hoping they will recover; they will not
  • Don't plant new cucurbits next to old infected crops

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if many young plants show mosaic or curling, so they can confirm the virus and advise on controlling the insects that spread it.

Prevention

  • Use healthy seedlings, raised under insect-proof net in the nursery where possible
  • Grow tolerant varieties recommended for your area
  • Use silver reflective mulch at planting to repel aphids; it works until the leaves cover it
  • Grow a few rows of maize or sorghum around the field as a barrier
  • Keep the field and its borders free of weeds
  • Avoid planting cucurbits next to tomato, chilli, okra or cotton fields with many whiteflies

Problems That Look Similar

  • Downy mildew

    Angular yellow patches that turn brown, with grey-purple fuzz underneath; leaves are not crinkled or curled.

  • Mite or thrips damage

    Leaves are bronzed, silvery or speckled, with tiny mites, webs or thrips underneath; no clear mosaic pattern.

  • Herbicide drift damage

    Twisted, strap-like leaves appear on many plants at once after spraying nearby, and do not spread from plant to plant.
