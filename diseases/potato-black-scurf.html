Black Scurf and Stem Canker of Potato

Hard black crusts like dirt stuck on potato skin that do not wash off; brown cankers on stems.

Overview

Black scurf is caused by a soil fungus that forms hard black lumps (sclerotia) on the potato skin. They look like dried mud but do not wash off. The same fungus attacks young sprouts, stems and stolons underground and causes brown sunken cankers, which is called stem canker.

The black lumps stay in the skin and do not rot the flesh, but they lower the market value and carry the disease to the next crop if such tubers are planted. Surveys in Pakistan have found black scurf very common on market potatoes. Stem canker is worst when seed is planted into cold, wet soil and sprouts come up slowly.

Symptoms

  • Hard black or dark brown crusts on the tuber skin, from flat specks to raised lumps, that do not wash off
  • Gaps in the field where sprouts were killed or came up late and weak
  • Reddish-brown sunken cankers on underground stems and stolons
  • Upper leaves roll upward and turn yellow or purple
  • Small green 'aerial tubers' forming on the stem above the ground
  • Misshapen, cracked or small tubers, often in clusters near the stem

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Rhizoctonia solani (mainly strain AG-3), carried on seed tubers and in soil.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Planting seed tubers that carry the black lumps, which is often more important than soil
  • The fungus also lives in soil and on crop debris
  • Cold, wet soil after planting slows sprout emergence and favours infection
  • Deep planting and over-watering early in the season
  • Leaving tubers in the ground for a long time after the vines have died

What to Do

  • Wash a few tubers: if black crusts stay after washing, it is black scurf
  • Dig up weak plants and look for brown cankers on stems and stolons
  • Keep scurfy tubers separate and do not keep them for seed
  • Ask your agriculture officer about approved seed-tuber treatment for the next crop

Don't

  • Do not plant seed tubers that have black scurf

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if many plants are missing or weak after emergence, or if most of your tubers have black crusts and you plan to keep seed.

Prevention

  • Plant certified seed tubers that are free of black crusts
  • Treat seed tubers with an approved fungicide if advised by your agriculture department
  • Let seed tubers sprout before planting and avoid planting into cold, wet soil
  • Plant at a shallow depth and avoid over-watering early in the season
  • Rotate with non-potato crops for two to three years
  • Harvest soon after the vines die instead of leaving tubers long in the soil

Problems That Look Similar

  • Common scab

    Rough, corky, raised or pitted spots that are part of the skin, not black lumps sitting on the skin.

  • Soil stuck on tubers

    Dirt washes off easily; black scurf stays after washing and rubbing.

  • Silver scurf

    Silvery-grey shiny patches on the skin rather than black crusts.
