Cotton Mealybug

White, cotton-wool-like insects in clusters on stems, shoots and bolls; sticky leaves and black mould.

Overview

The cotton mealybug is a small, oval, wingless insect covered in white wax that looks like a bit of cotton wool. It lives in colonies on stems, shoots, leaves and bolls and sucks sap. It feeds on a very wide range of plants, including cotton, okra, brinjal, tomato, ornamental plants and many weeds.

It appeared as a serious pest of cotton in Pakistan in 2005 and spread through most cotton areas within a year or two. Heavy attack makes plants bushy and stunted, with crinkled leaves and fewer, smaller bolls. Finding and removing the first patches early is much easier than controlling an attack that has spread through the field.

Symptoms

  • White waxy insects in clusters on shoot tips, stems, under leaves and on bolls
  • Dark lines on the back of the white females
  • White cottony egg sacs among the insects
  • Sticky honeydew and black sooty mould on leaves
  • Bushy shoots, crinkled or twisted leaves and stunted plants
  • Attacked plants in patches, often starting at the field edge
  • Fewer, small or misshapen bolls; plants may dry early

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Phenacoccus solenopsis (sap-sucking mealybug).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Tiny young crawlers move from plant to plant and are carried by wind, rain and irrigation water
  • Crawlers stick to people, animals, tools and machinery moving through infested fields
  • Weeds on field borders and along water channels carry it between crops
  • Ants protect mealybugs from their natural enemies in return for the honeydew
  • It breeds fast in warm weather, with many generations in a year

What to Do

  • Mark infested plants and check the plants around them every few days
  • Cut out heavily infested plants or branches early, put them in a bag and burn or bury them away from the field
  • Remove weeds from field borders and water channels
  • Visit clean fields first, and clean tools and clothes after working in infested fields
  • Contact your agriculture officer for advice on whether a spray is needed and which product is approved

Don't

  • Don't throw uprooted weeds or infested plants into water channels or on field borders
  • Don't spray broad-spectrum insecticides again and again; they kill the tiny wasps and lady beetles that attack mealybugs

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer when you first find mealybug colonies in your field, or when patches spread to many plants.

Prevention

  • Keep field borders, roadsides and water channels free of weeds
  • Destroy cotton sticks and crop leftovers after harvest, or keep them away from new crops
  • Check field edges regularly from early growth
  • Don't move infested plant material between fields
  • Protect natural enemies such as the parasitic wasp Aenasius and lady beetles
  • Clean farm machinery before moving it out of infested fields

Problems That Look Similar

  • Whitefly

    Whiteflies have wings and fly up when disturbed; mealybugs crawl slowly and are covered in thick white wax.

  • Mango mealybug

    A bigger mealybug that crawls up mango tree trunks in late winter and spring; it is not a pest of cotton.

  • Powdery mildew

    A thin white powder on the leaf surface that wipes off, with no insect bodies underneath.
