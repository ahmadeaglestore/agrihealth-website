Brown Spot of Sugarcane

Oval reddish-brown spots with a yellow halo on older leaves; badly hit leaves turn yellow and dry.

Overview

Brown spot is a fungal leaf disease of sugarcane. It makes small reddish-brown to dark brown oval spots, each with a yellow ring (halo) around it, mostly on the older leaves. When there are many spots, whole leaves turn yellow and dry early, and the field can look burnt.

It is usually a minor disease, but on a susceptible variety in a wet season it can become severe. High rainfall and high humidity favour it, so it mostly shows during and after the monsoon. The variety grown makes the biggest difference: a weak variety can be badly hit while the field next to it stays clean.

Symptoms

  • Small brown specks on the leaf blade
  • The specks grow into oval reddish-brown to dark brown spots with a yellow halo around them
  • Spots often lie in lines along the leaf
  • Mostly on older, mature leaves; young leaves are usually less affected
  • Many spots join, and leaves turn yellow to pale yellow and dry early
  • In a bad attack the whole field looks dry or burnt

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Cercospora longipes (fungus).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • The fungus spreads from spotted leaves and infected leaf trash to nearby plants
  • High rainfall and high humidity
  • The monsoon and the weeks after it
  • Growing a susceptible variety

What to Do

  • Check the older leaves for oval brown spots with a yellow halo, and note which variety is affected
  • Strip off badly spotted lower leaves and burn or bury them away from the field
  • Drain standing water so the field does not stay wet
  • If spots are spreading fast over many plants, ask your agriculture officer whether any spray is needed

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if leaves are drying over large parts of the field during or after the monsoon. They can confirm brown spot and suggest a better variety for your area.

Prevention

  • Grow a variety recommended for your area, and replace one that gets brown spot badly
  • Do not plant one variety on all your land
  • Collect and destroy spotted leaves and trash after harvest
  • Keep the field well drained

Problems That Look Similar

  • Rust

    Long pustules that release brown or orange powder when rubbed; brown spot leaves no powder.

  • Red rot (leaf midrib lesions)

    Red streaks lie on the thick midrib, not oval spots on the blade, and the split cane is red with white bands.
