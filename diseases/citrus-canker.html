Citrus Canker

Raised, corky brown spots with yellow halos on citrus leaves, twigs and fruit.

Overview

Citrus canker is a bacterial disease of almost all citrus types. It makes rough, corky spots on leaves, twigs and fruit. Spotted fruit loses market value, and in bad attacks leaves and fruit drop early.

The disease is common in Pakistan's citrus areas. A survey in Sargodha district found it in many kinnow orchards, and pummelo (chakotra) and grapefruit were among the most severely damaged types. It spreads most in the rainy season, when wind-driven rain splashes the bacteria onto young leaves and green fruit.

Symptoms

  • Small, oily, water-soaked spots, first on the underside of young leaves
  • Spots become raised, rough and corky and can be seen on both sides of the leaf
  • A yellow halo often surrounds the leaf spots
  • Older spots turn brown with a crater-like centre
  • Similar raised, corky spots on green twigs and on the fruit skin
  • In heavy attacks: leaf drop, early fruit drop and twig die-back

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Xanthomonas citri subsp. citri (bacterium).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Wind-driven rain, and splash from rain or overhead watering
  • Wounds made by citrus leafminer, thorns and pruning let the bacteria in
  • Infected nursery plants and budwood, and contaminated tools and clothing
  • Young, soft leaves and green fruit in warm, wet weather are most at risk

What to Do

  • Prune out badly spotted twigs in dry weather; collect and burn fallen spotted leaves and twigs
  • Clean pruning tools before moving to the next tree
  • Control citrus leafminer on young flush, because its tunnels help canker enter
  • Ask your agriculture officer about a protective copper-based spray and the right timing for your area

Don't

  • Don't prune or work in the orchard while trees are wet
  • Don't take plants or budwood from orchards or nurseries with canker

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer or the citrus research institute if canker appears in a new orchard or nursery, or if spots are spreading fast on fruit after rain. Plants from a nursery with canker should not be sold or planted.

Prevention

  • Plant only healthy, certified nursery plants
  • Plant windbreaks around the orchard
  • Water at ground level; avoid sprinklers that wet the leaves
  • Prune and remove spotted parts before the monsoon
  • Keep trees strong with balanced fertiliser and regular irrigation
  • For new plantings, choose less susceptible types as advised locally

Problems That Look Similar

  • Citrus scab

    Raised, wart-like spots, often on only one side of the leaf and making leaves twisted, without a yellow halo.

  • Citrus leafminer damage

    Winding silvery tunnels inside the leaf, not raised corky spots.
