Sugarcane Top Borer

Rows of small holes in young leaves, red tunnel in the midrib, dried central shoot and bunchy top.

Overview

The top borer is one of the main borer pests of sugarcane in Pakistan. The adult is a white moth. Its caterpillar enters the leaf midrib, tunnels down into the growing point of the cane and kills it.

When the growing point dies, the central shoot dries (dead heart) and side shoots grow from the top, giving a bunchy top. The pest has several generations from spring to autumn; the summer generations (around July to September) usually do the most damage to growing canes.

Symptoms

  • Rows of small round 'shot holes' across the young unfolding leaves
  • A reddish-brown tunnel along the midrib of the leaf
  • The central shoot dries (dead heart); in grown canes it is reddish-brown and hard to pull out
  • Side shoots sprout below the dead top, giving a bunched, broom-like top
  • Smooth white or cream caterpillar with a red line down the back inside the top of the cane
  • Egg masses covered with buff-coloured hairs under the top leaves

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Scirpophaga excerptalis (syn. S. nivella), a white moth whose caterpillar bores down the top shoot.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • White moths fly to fields and lay egg masses on the underside of top leaves
  • Several generations a year, from spring to autumn
  • Leaving dead hearts and infested canes in the field lets caterpillars complete their life
  • Ratoon and late-planted crops next to infested fields

What to Do

  • Check canes for shot holes, red midrib tunnels and dead hearts, and count how many canes are attacked
  • Cut out dead hearts and attacked tops close to the ground, and destroy them so the caterpillar inside is killed
  • Collect and destroy egg masses from the underside of top leaves
  • Ask your agriculture department about Trichogramma (egg parasite) cards and pheromone traps
  • If many canes are attacked, ask your agriculture officer whether a treatment is needed and which product is approved

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if attacked canes are increasing quickly or reach about one in ten canes, which is the level the Punjab pest warning service uses to recommend action.

Prevention

  • Plant healthy setts and a variety recommended for your area
  • Install Trichogramma cards from early in the season, as advised by your agriculture department
  • Use pheromone traps to catch moths and to know when they are flying
  • Remove and destroy dead hearts regularly during the season
  • Clean up and destroy infested trash and stubble after harvest

Problems That Look Similar

  • Early shoot borer

    Attacks young shoots before canes form; the dead heart pulls out easily and smells bad.

  • Red rot

    Leaves dry from the third or fourth leaf and the split cane is red with white bands, without a caterpillar tunnel.
