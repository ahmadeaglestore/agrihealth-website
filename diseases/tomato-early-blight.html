Early Blight

Brown spots with rings like a target, starting on the older lower leaves.

Overview

Early blight is a common fungal disease of tomato and potato. It usually starts on the oldest, lowest leaves and slowly moves up the plant.

It rarely kills plants quickly, but it can strip away many leaves, which weakens the plant, exposes fruit to sunscald and lowers yield.

Symptoms

  • Brown to black spots with rings inside, like a target
  • A yellow ring (halo) around the spots
  • Spots appearing first on older, lower leaves
  • Lower leaves turning yellow, drying and falling off
  • Dark, sunken spots on stems and on tomato fruit near the stem

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Alternaria species (A. solani, A. linariae).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Spores spread by wind, rain splash and overhead watering
  • Infected plant debris left in the soil
  • Warm weather with repeated wetting and drying of leaves
  • Stressed or poorly fed plants are more affected

What to Do

  • Remove badly spotted lower leaves and take them out of the field (do not leave them on the ground)
  • Water at the base of plants instead of over the leaves
  • Ask your agriculture officer or a trusted local adviser whether a fungicide is needed
  • Feed plants properly; stressed plants suffer more

When to call an expert: Get advice if the disease is moving up the plant quickly or fruit is being damaged.

Prevention

  • Rotate crops; avoid planting tomato or potato in the same place every year
  • Use mulch to stop soil splashing onto lower leaves
  • Stake or cage tomato plants and space them for good air flow
  • Remove and destroy plant debris after harvest
  • Use healthy seed and transplants, and tolerant varieties where available

Problems That Look Similar

  • Late blight

    Large dark, wet-looking patches that spread very fast, often with white growth underneath.

  • Septoria leaf spot

    Many small round spots with pale centres and no target rings.
