Fusarium Basal Rot of Onion

Leaf tips yellow and die back; the bulb base and roots rot brown, often with white mould, also in storage.

Overview

Fusarium basal rot is a soil-borne fungal disease of onion and garlic. The fungus enters through the roots and the base of the bulb (the basal plate, where the roots grow), often through wounds. It rots the roots and the bulb base, and then moves up into the bulb scales.

The fungus survives in the soil as thick-walled resting spores, which become active when the soil is warm. Leaf yellowing can show in the field, but many infected bulbs look healthy at harvest and only rot later in storage, so the disease causes losses both in the field and in the store.

Symptoms

  • Leaf tips turn yellow, curl and die back, moving down the leaf
  • Plants may wilt and pull out easily because the roots have rotted
  • The base of the bulb, where the roots grow, turns reddish-brown and soft
  • White fungal growth may be seen on the bulb base or between the scales
  • Cut bulbs show brown, watery rot spreading up from the base; scales may look reddish or purple
  • Stored bulbs rot from the base and become soft and shrunken; garlic cloves become sunken and wrinkled

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cepae (soil-borne fungus).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • The fungus lives in the soil and becomes active when the soil is warm
  • It enters through wounds made by soil insects such as maggots, by tools, hoeing or fertiliser burn
  • Growing onion, garlic or leeks in the same field year after year builds up the fungus
  • Some crops such as maize, tomato and oats can carry the fungus without showing signs
  • Soil carried on tools and shoes, and infected sets or cloves, can move it to new fields

What to Do

  • Pull out yellowing plants with rotten bases, put them in a bag and take them out of the field
  • Avoid injuring bulbs and roots during hoeing and digging
  • At harvest, sort bulbs carefully and remove any with soft or discoloured bases before storing
  • Dry (cure) bulbs well in shade with good air flow, then store them in a cool, dry, airy place
  • Ask your agriculture officer about seed or set treatment with an approved fungicide for the next crop

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if many plants in the field are yellowing with rotten bases, or if a large share of stored onions is rotting.

Prevention

  • Rotate: keep onion, garlic and leeks out of the field for at least three to four years
  • Avoid planting onion in fields where basal rot was a problem before
  • Grow resistant or tolerant varieties recommended for your area
  • Use healthy seed, sets and cloves, and plant only strong cloves
  • Protect plants from insect, fertiliser and tool injury
  • Store onions as cool and dry as possible with good air flow, because warm stores increase rotting

Problems That Look Similar

  • Pink root

    Roots turn pink to purple and shrivel, but the bulb base stays firm and does not rot.

  • Bacterial soft rot

    Bulb scales become watery and slimy with a bad smell, usually starting from the neck or a wound, not from the base with white mould.

  • Water or heat stress

    Leaf tips dry evenly across the field, but the roots and bulb base are white and firm.
