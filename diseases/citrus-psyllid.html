Asian Citrus Psyllid

Tiny brown insects sitting tail-up on new citrus shoots; yellow-orange young with white waxy threads.

Overview

The Asian citrus psyllid is a small brown sap-sucking insect of citrus. It damages new shoots directly, but its main danger is that it carries the bacterium that causes citrus greening (HLB).

Psyllids lay eggs only on soft new flush. In kinnow orchards around Sargodha, adult psyllids were most numerous in April and September, during the spring and autumn flush. Watching new flush in these months is the key to control.

Symptoms

  • Small (about 3 mm) mottled brown insects feeding head-down with the body raised at an angle
  • Tiny yellow-orange eggs tucked in the folds of new leaves
  • Flat yellow-orange young (nymphs) on new shoots, with white waxy threads
  • Sticky honeydew and black sooty mould on shoots and leaves
  • Tips of new leaves curl back, twist or look deformed

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Diaphorina citri (small sap-sucking insect).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Adults fly between trees and orchards
  • Moved on nursery plants and on curry leaf (Murraya) plants
  • Numbers rise when trees push new flush, with peaks in spring and autumn
  • Ants that feed on honeydew protect psyllids from their natural enemies

What to Do

  • Check new flush every one to two weeks in spring and autumn; look for eggs and young with a hand lens
  • Tap shoots over a white board or paper to see adults, and use yellow sticky traps to know when they arrive
  • Ask your agriculture officer when to treat and which approved product to use, especially during flush
  • Remove curry leaf and other Murraya plants near the orchard

Don't

  • Don't bring citrus or curry leaf plants from unknown nurseries

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer when you find psyllids on new flush, and report any trees showing greening symptoms.

Prevention

  • Buy certified, insect-free nursery plants
  • Protect natural enemies such as the tiny wasp Tamarixia radiata and hover fly larvae
  • Control ants on the trees
  • Time fertiliser and pruning so that big flushes do not come when psyllids are most active
  • Windbreaks around the orchard can reduce psyllid movement

Problems That Look Similar

  • Aphids

    Soft green or black insects in groups on new shoots; they do not sit tail-up at an angle and their young have no white waxy threads.

  • Citrus mealybug

    White, cotton-like waxy insects in clusters, often on fruit and stems.
