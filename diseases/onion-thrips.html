Onion Thrips

Silvery-white streaks on onion leaves with tiny yellow insects hiding in the leaf folds.

Overview

Onion thrips are tiny, slender, pale yellow to light brown insects, about the size of a comma. They hide in the leaf folds and near the neck of onion and garlic plants and scrape the leaf surface to feed on the sap. The same insect is an important early-season pest of cotton seedlings in Pakistan and also attacks cabbage and cauliflower.

Feeding leaves silvery-white streaks; heavily attacked fields look silvery from a distance, leaf tips dry and bulbs stay small. Thrips are worst in hot, dry weather and can build up very fast. Their damage also opens the way for leaf diseases like purple blotch, and they can spread iris yellow spot virus in onion.

Symptoms

  • Silvery-white streaks and patches on leaves
  • Tiny pale yellow or light brown insects inside the leaf folds and near the neck
  • Leaf tips turn yellow, then brown, and dry
  • Heavily attacked fields look silvery from a distance
  • Small or misshapen bulbs
  • On cotton seedlings, leaves become wrinkled and distorted with shiny white patches

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Thrips tabaci (onion thrips, also called cotton seedling thrips).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Hot, dry weather favours fast build-up; rain and sprinkler water knock numbers down
  • Thrips move in from wheat and other crops, weeds and old onion leftovers when those dry or are harvested
  • Many generations a year; females can lay eggs without mating
  • Thick-necked, tightly folded varieties give more hiding places
  • Repeated broad insecticide sprays kill natural enemies such as predatory mites, minute pirate bugs and lacewings

What to Do

  • Pull apart the leaves of several plants in different parts of the field and look for thrips in the folds near the neck
  • Put up yellow or white sticky traps to monitor them
  • Keep the field watered on time; avoid drought stress
  • Remove weeds around the field
  • If thrips are many and damage is spreading, ask your agriculture officer which treatment is approved

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if thrips numbers rise quickly, the field turns silvery, or cotton seedlings show wrinkled leaves with shiny patches.

Prevention

  • Do not plant onion next to wheat or other crops that are about to be harvested or dry, if possible
  • Remove onion leftovers, volunteer plants and weeds before planting
  • Choose varieties recommended for your area; open, glossy-leaved types are less damaged
  • Scout every week in hot, dry weather
  • Protect natural enemies by avoiding unnecessary sprays

Problems That Look Similar

  • Purple blotch

    Oval purple-brown spots with rings and a yellow border, rather than silvery streaks.

  • Chilli thrips

    A different thrips species that mainly curls and scars chilli leaves upward.

  • Mite damage

    Fine speckling and sometimes webbing, with tiny mites rather than slender thrips.
