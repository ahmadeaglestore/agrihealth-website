Rice Hispa

White parallel streaks scraped on rice leaves and blister-like mines, with small dark spiny beetles.

Overview

Rice hispa is a small, squarish, dark blue-black beetle covered with spines. Adults scrape off the green upper layer of rice leaves, leaving white streaks. The grubs (larvae) live inside the leaf and eat tunnels, making whitish blisters, often near the leaf tip.

Hispa is more of a problem in wet rice areas and in the rainy season. It has been recorded in Pakistan, but it is not reported as one of the main rice pests here. Grassy weeds, dense planting, heavy fertiliser, and heavy early rain followed by a dry spell favour it. A badly attacked field looks burnt.

Symptoms

  • White streaks running along the leaf, parallel to the midrib, where adults have scraped off the green layer
  • Whitish, see-through blotches or blisters inside the leaf, often near the tip, where grubs are mining
  • Small blue-black beetles covered with spines sitting on the leaves
  • Damaged leaves turn whitish and papery and dry up
  • Heavily attacked fields look scorched or burnt from a distance

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Dicladispa armigera (spiny leaf beetle; adults scrape leaves, grubs mine inside them).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Adult beetles fly in from grassy weeds and nearby fields
  • Grassy weeds in and around the field act as other food plants
  • Heavy fertiliser use and dense, leafy crops
  • Heavy rain early in the season followed by unusually dry weather, high humidity and little difference between day and night temperatures

What to Do

  • Check the leaves for white streaks and look for the spiny beetles
  • Hold a damaged leaf against the light to see grubs or pupae inside the blisters
  • Catch adults with a sweep net again and again, and destroy them
  • If the attack is heavy, skip the next nitrogen top dressing
  • Spray only if beetles are many (Indian guidance uses about two adults per hill), and only with an insecticide recommended by your agriculture department

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if many leaves show white streaks and you find several beetles per hill, especially in a young crop.

Prevention

  • Remove grassy weeds from the field and bunds
  • Avoid too much nitrogen fertiliser
  • Protect natural enemies such as small parasitic wasps and predatory bugs by avoiding unneeded sprays
  • Scout the crop regularly in the weeks after transplanting

Problems That Look Similar

  • Rice leaffolder

    Leaves are folded lengthwise with a green caterpillar inside; hispa leaves are not folded and the beetles sit in the open.

  • Thrips damage

    Leaf tips roll and turn yellow-silvery in young crops; the insects are tiny and slender, without spines.

  • Bacterial leaf streak

    Water-soaked streaks with amber ooze droplets and no insects.
