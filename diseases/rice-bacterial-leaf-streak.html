Bacterial Leaf Streak of Rice

Narrow, water-soaked streaks between the leaf veins that turn yellow-brown, often with tiny amber droplets.

Overview

Bacterial leaf streak is a bacterial disease of rice leaves. It is related to bacterial leaf blight, but instead of drying the leaf from its edges it makes narrow streaks between the veins, anywhere on the leaf. Many streaks can join so that the whole leaf turns brown and dries.

The disease is recorded in Pakistan and is widespread in India. It is a kharif problem: rain, high humidity and warm to hot weather (about 28–40 °C) favour it. The bacteria enter through natural openings and through wounds, including those made by leaffolder and hispa feeding.

Symptoms

  • Small, dark green, water-soaked streaks between the leaf veins, anywhere on the leaf blade
  • Streaks grow longer, turn yellow-orange to brown and look see-through when the leaf is held against the light
  • Tiny amber or yellow droplets of bacterial ooze on the streaks, easiest to see in the early morning
  • Streak edges stay straight because the veins stop them from spreading sideways
  • Streaks join into large brown areas and the leaves dry out

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzicola (bacterium).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Infected seed carries the bacteria to the next crop
  • Rain splash, wind-driven rain and irrigation water move the bacteria
  • Leaves touching and rubbing together spread it within the field
  • Wounds from insects such as leaffolder and hispa help the bacteria enter
  • Infected straw and stubble can carry it over
  • Rain, high humidity and warm to hot weather

What to Do

  • Hold a leaf against the light: thin see-through streaks between the veins point to leaf streak
  • Look for small amber droplets on the streaks early in the morning
  • Hold back extra nitrogen while the disease is active
  • Keep leaffolder and hispa damage in check, because their wounds let the bacteria in
  • Contact your agriculture officer to confirm; sprays have only limited effect on bacterial diseases

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if streaks with ooze are spreading over many plants, especially before flowering.

Prevention

  • Use clean, certified seed; never keep seed from a diseased field
  • Grow a variety that is less affected in your area
  • Treat seed before sowing as advised by your agriculture department
  • Use balanced fertiliser and split the nitrogen
  • Do not let water from an infected field flow into healthy fields
  • Remove infected stubble, straw and volunteer rice plants after harvest

Problems That Look Similar

  • Bacterial leaf blight

    Drying starts from the leaf tips and edges with a wavy border; leaf streak makes narrow, straight-edged streaks between veins anywhere on the leaf.

  • Rice hispa damage

    White scraped streaks with small dark spiny beetles on the leaves; no water-soaked streaks or ooze.

  • Rice leaffolder

    Leaves folded lengthwise with a caterpillar inside and white scraped streaks; no ooze.
