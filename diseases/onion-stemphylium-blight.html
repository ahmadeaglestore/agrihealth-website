Stemphylium Leaf Blight of Onion

Small yellow spots grow into long tan-brown patches, mostly near leaf tips; leaves dry from the tip.

Overview

Stemphylium leaf blight is a fungal leaf disease of onion. It starts as small, light-yellow, water-soaked spots that grow into long, tan to brown patches, mostly towards the leaf tips. As the spots age, their centres turn dark olive-brown to black with fungal spores. It often comes together with purple blotch, and the two can be hard to tell apart.

Spots join and the leaves dry from the tip, so less green leaf is left to feed the bulb. A study at the Ayub Agricultural Research Institute, Faisalabad, found that rain and humid weather increased the disease and that it became more severe as temperatures fell. Thrips can also carry the spores from plant to plant.

Symptoms

  • Small, light-yellow, water-soaked spots on the leaves, often towards the tip
  • Spots grow into long, oval, tan to brown patches
  • Centres of older spots turn dark olive-brown to black from fungal spores
  • Spots join and the leaf dies back from the tip; leaves look burnt
  • Usually no clear purple colour or target rings (those point to purple blotch)
  • Badly hit fields look brown from a distance and plants lose leaves early

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Stemphylium vesicarium (sexual stage Pleospora allii).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • The fungus survives on onion crop remains, volunteer onions and some weeds
  • Infected seed and nursery plants can bring it into a new field
  • Spores spread by wind, rain splash and thrips
  • Leaves staying wet for long periods from rain, dew or fog in mild weather
  • Humid, rainy spells in the cooler part of the season

What to Do

  • Check older leaves and leaf tips for yellow-brown spots, and look for purple spots with rings as well, as both diseases often come together
  • Keep thrips under control, as they carry spores and damage the leaves
  • Irrigate in the morning, not in the evening, so leaves do not stay wet at night
  • Contact your agriculture officer about whether a fungicide is needed; this fungus can become resistant, so do not keep spraying the same type of product

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if brown, blighted leaves are spreading quickly through the field, or if you cannot tell it apart from purple blotch.

Prevention

  • Use healthy seed and disease-free nursery plants
  • Remove or plough under onion crop remains after harvest, and destroy volunteer onions
  • Rotate: grow another crop for at least one year before onion again, and longer if purple blotch is also a problem
  • Control weeds in and around the field early in the season
  • Give proper spacing so leaves dry faster

Problems That Look Similar

  • Purple blotch

    Oval spots with a purple colour, target-like rings and a yellow border.

  • Onion thrips damage

    Silvery-white streaks and patches with tiny insects in the leaf folds, not brown spots with dark centres.

  • Iris yellow spot virus

    Straw-coloured diamond or eye-shaped patches, sometimes with green islands, and no dark fungal growth in the centre.
