Inflorescence Rot (Khamedj) of Date Palm

Brown or rusty patches on unopened spathes; inside, the flower strands are rotten and covered with powder.

Overview

Inflorescence rot, known as khamedj in North Africa and the Middle East, is a fungal disease of the date palm's flower clusters and the sheaths (spathes) around them. The main fungus is Mauginiella scaettae, but other fungi such as Fusarium, Thielaviopsis and Diplodia can cause similar rots. Infection starts early, while the young spathe is still hidden among the leaf bases. The first sign is a brown or rusty patch on the outside of the unopened spathe. When the spathe splits, the flowers and strands are partly or fully rotten and covered with white powdery spores. Badly affected spathes may not open at all.

Both male and female palms are attacked. The disease is most serious in humid areas and after long spells of rain or a cold, humid winter, and it is common in neglected orchards. It can come back on the same palm every year, and it spreads during pollination through infected male flowers. In Pakistan, spathes come out in late winter and spring, and a report on date palm in Pakistan notes that rain and high humidity at flowering favour rotting of the flower clusters. Which fungi cause it here is not well recorded, so ask the agriculture department or the Date Palm Research Institute to confirm it.

Symptoms

  • Brown or rusty patches on the outside of unopened spathes, clearest on the inner face
  • When the spathe splits, flowers and strands are brown, rotten and partly or fully destroyed
  • A white to creamy powdery layer of spores on the rotten parts
  • Badly affected spathes stay closed and dry out
  • No fruit sets on affected strands, so bunches are small or missing
  • The same palms are affected year after year

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Mauginiella scaettae (khamedj fungus); other fungi such as Fusarium, Thielaviopsis and Diplodia can also rot flower clusters.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Infection starts early, while spathes are still hidden in the leaf bases
  • It spreads during pollination through infected male flower strands
  • The fungus survives in old infected spathes and flower remains left on the palm
  • Long spells of rain, high humidity and a cold, humid winter favour it
  • Neglected palms with old spathes, fruit stalks and dead fronds left on the crown have more disease

What to Do

  • Inspect spathes as they come out, especially after rain
  • Cut off infected spathes and flower clusters as soon as you see them; collect them and burn or bury them away from the orchard
  • Do not use pollen or strands from male palms that show rot
  • Clean and disinfect cutting tools between palms
  • Ask your agriculture officer or the Date Palm Research Institute which fungicide is recommended for affected palms and when; treatment is usually given after harvest and again before the spathes come out

Don't

  • Don't leave rotten spathes hanging on the palm or lying under it
  • Don't pollinate with strands from male palms that have rotten spathes

When to call an expert: Contact the agriculture department or the Date Palm Research Institute if several palms have rotting spathes, or the same palms are affected every year, so the fungus can be confirmed and treatment planned before the next flowering.

Prevention

  • After harvest, clean the crown: remove old spathe remains, fruit stalks and dead fronds
  • Keep the orchard well looked after, with good drainage and no standing water around the palms
  • Take pollen only from healthy male palms
  • Where advised, prefer less susceptible varieties; FAO lists Medjool and Khadrawy among the more susceptible ones
  • Mark affected palms and give them early care before the next flowering

Problems That Look Similar

  • Black scorch of date palm

    Black, hard, burnt-looking patches, also on fronds and the growing heart; khamedj gives brown or rusty patches on spathes, with white powder inside.

  • Poor pollination

    Flowers look healthy but fruits do not set or drop early; there is no brown rot and no powder on the spathe.
