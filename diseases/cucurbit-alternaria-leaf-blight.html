Alternaria Leaf Blight of Cucurbits

Brown spots with rings and yellow halos on older leaves near the crown; leaves curl, dry and fall.

Overview

Alternaria leaf blight is a fungal leaf disease of cucurbits. It mainly attacks muskmelon (kharbooza) and watermelon (tarbooz), and less often cucumber, pumpkin, squash and gourds. Spots start on older leaves near the crown (the centre of the plant) and then move out along the vine.

It is favoured by warm weather with frequent rain, high humidity and overhead watering, so it is most damaging in rainy spells. Spots join into large dead areas, leaves curl and fall, and the bare fruit get sunscald and lose quality.

Symptoms

  • Small, water-soaked or yellow-brown spots on older leaves near the centre of the plant
  • Spots grow into brown patches, often with a yellow-green halo
  • Larger spots show dark rings inside, like a target
  • Spots join into large dead areas; leaves cup upward and die
  • Vines lose many leaves, and exposed fruit get sunscald
  • Some fruit may rot, often starting from the blossom end

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Alternaria cucumerina (fungus that survives on crop remains).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • The fungus survives for one to two years on crop remains in the soil
  • Spores are carried by wind and splashing water from plant to plant
  • Warm weather with frequent rain, high humidity and long leaf wetness
  • Overhead watering, especially late in the day
  • Cucurbit fields close to each other, and growing cucurbits in the same field again and again

What to Do

  • Check older leaves near the centre of the plant regularly, so the disease is found early
  • Remove the worst spotted older leaves and take them out of the field
  • Water in furrows or by drip; avoid overhead watering, especially late in the day
  • Ask your agriculture officer whether a fungicide spray is needed and which product is approved

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if spots are moving quickly from the older leaves to the whole vine, or vines are losing many leaves before the fruit is ready.

Prevention

  • Rotate out of all cucurbits for at least two to three years
  • Use certified, disease-free seed and resistant varieties where available
  • Plough crop remains deep or remove them after harvest
  • Give good spacing for air flow, and keep tunnels well ventilated
  • Keep some distance between cucurbit fields where possible

Problems That Look Similar

  • Anthracnose

    Spot centres crack into holes, and fruit get sunken spots with pink ooze in wet weather.

  • Downy mildew

    Yellow, angular patches bounded by veins, with grey-purple fuzz under the leaf and no target rings.

  • Bacterial leaf spot

    Small water-soaked spots with yellow halos that can look angular and leave ragged holes; no target rings.
