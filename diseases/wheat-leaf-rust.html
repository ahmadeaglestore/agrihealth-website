Leaf (Brown) Rust

Small, round, orange-brown powdery spots scattered across wheat leaves.

Overview

Leaf rust, also called brown rust, is the most widespread rust disease of wheat. It is caused by a fungus that forms small, round, orange-brown pustules on the leaves.

It usually appears a little later in the season than yellow rust and prefers slightly warmer weather. When it attacks the upper leaves before the grain has filled, it can noticeably reduce yield.

Symptoms

  • Small, round to oval orange-brown pustules scattered on the leaf, not in stripes
  • Pustules mostly on the upper side of the leaf
  • Orange-brown powder rubs off on your fingers
  • Yellowing around groups of pustules
  • Late in the season, pustules may turn dark brown to black

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Puccinia triticina.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Wind-blown spores, which can travel long distances
  • Moderate temperatures (about 15–22 °C) with dew or rain on the leaves
  • Susceptible varieties and volunteer wheat plants

What to Do

  • Check how many plants are affected and whether the upper leaves are infected
  • Ask your local agriculture officer whether a fungicide is worthwhile at your crop stage and which product is approved
  • Note the variety you planted; resistance is the cheapest protection next season

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture office if rust is spreading onto the flag leaf before the grain has filled.

Prevention

  • Grow resistant varieties recommended for your area
  • Remove volunteer wheat plants between seasons
  • Sow at the recommended time
  • Scout regularly from tillering onwards

Problems That Look Similar

  • Yellow (stripe) rust

    Yellow pustules arranged in stripes rather than scattered spots.

  • Powdery mildew

    White to grey cotton-like growth instead of orange powder.

  • Stem (black) rust

    Long, dark reddish-brown pustules on stems and both sides of leaves that tear the surface.

Sources
