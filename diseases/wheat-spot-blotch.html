Spot Blotch (Helminthosporium Leaf Blight) of Wheat

Dark brown oval spots on wheat leaves that grow and join until leaves dry; worse in warm, humid weather.

Overview

Spot blotch, also called Helminthosporium leaf blight or simply leaf blight, is a fungal disease of wheat. It makes oval, light to dark brown spots on the leaves, which grow and join until the leaf dries up. It can also attack leaf sheaths, stem nodes and the ear. The same fungus can cause root rot and a dark tip on the grain (black point).

It is a disease of the warmer, more humid wheat areas of South Asia. In Pakistan, surveys in 2005, 2006 and 2008 found it in many agro-ecological zones; the worst outbreak was recorded in Punjab in 2005, and it is also reported from Sindh, where winters are warmer. It usually builds up late in the season, when temperatures rise and dew or rain keeps the leaves wet for long periods.

Symptoms

  • Small dark brown spots on the lower leaves at first
  • Spots grow into oval or long blotches, light to dark brown, sometimes with a yellow edge
  • Spots join and large parts of the leaf turn brown and dry
  • Spots can also appear on leaf sheaths, stem nodes and the chaff of the ear
  • Grains may be shrivelled or have a dark tip (black point)
  • In bad cases the field looks burnt, as if it has dried early

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Bipolaris sorokiniana (also known as Cochliobolus sativus or Helminthosporium sativum).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • The fungus is carried on infected seed and survives on stubble, straw and in the soil
  • Some grasses and weeds, and rice in rice–wheat areas, may also carry it
  • Warm temperatures with high humidity and long leaf wetness from dew or rain favour it
  • Late-sown wheat meets warmer, humid weather at grain filling and usually suffers more
  • Unbalanced fertiliser (too little phosphorus or potash) and stressed crops are more affected

What to Do

  • Check the lower and middle leaves for dark brown oval spots and see whether they are reaching the flag leaf
  • Contact your agriculture officer to confirm, because several wheat leaf spots look alike, and ask whether a fungicide recommended by the agriculture department is needed
  • Do not keep seed from a badly affected field without asking about seed testing and seed treatment
  • Note the variety, sowing date and fertiliser used, to plan next season

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if dark brown spots are spreading quickly up the plant in warm, humid weather before the grain has filled.

Prevention

  • Sow a tolerant or resistant variety recommended for your area
  • Sow on time and avoid late sowing, especially in warmer areas
  • Use clean, certified seed, treated with an approved fungicide on the agriculture department's advice
  • Use balanced fertiliser with nitrogen, phosphorus and potash, ideally based on a soil test
  • Rotate crops, manage old stubble and control grass weeds

Problems That Look Similar

  • Tan spot

    Lighter tan spots with a clear yellow ring and a small dark point in the centre.

  • Septoria tritici blotch

    Pale tan patches with tiny black dots inside, mostly in cool, wet weather.

  • Leaf (brown) rust

    Raised orange-brown pustules with powder that rubs off on your fingers.
