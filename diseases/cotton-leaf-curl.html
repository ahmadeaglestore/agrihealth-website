Cotton Leaf Curl Disease

Curled leaves with thick, dark veins and small leaf-like growths underneath.

Overview

Cotton leaf curl disease (CLCuD) is a viral disease that has caused major losses in Pakistan and India. It is caused by a group of viruses carried from plant to plant by the whitefly.

There is no cure for an infected plant. Plants infected early stay stunted and produce far fewer bolls, so prevention and whitefly management are the main tools.

Symptoms

  • Leaves curling upwards or downwards
  • Veins that become thick and dark green
  • Small leaf-like outgrowths (enations) on the underside of leaves
  • Stunted plants with shortened gaps between nodes
  • Fewer flowers and bolls, and lower yield

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Begomovirus complex, spread by whitefly (Bemisia tabaci).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Whiteflies feeding on infected plants and then on healthy ones
  • Large whitefly populations in hot, dry weather
  • Virus sources such as infected cotton, volunteer plants and some weeds
  • It is not spread by touching plants or through soil

What to Do

  • Check plants for curled leaves and thick veins, and look for whiteflies under the leaves
  • Report early infections to your agriculture officer, who can advise on whitefly control in your area
  • Ask whether removing heavily infected young plants is advised in your area

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture office when you see leaf curl on young plants or a sudden increase in whiteflies.

Prevention

  • Grow varieties with tolerance to CLCuD recommended for your region
  • Sow at the recommended time
  • Control weeds and volunteer plants that host the virus or the whitefly
  • Monitor whitefly numbers regularly and follow local advice when they rise
  • Avoid planting next to heavily infected fields

Problems That Look Similar

  • Herbicide damage

    Twisted or cupped leaves without thick veins or enations, often in a pattern across the field.

  • Sucking pest damage

    Leaf cupping from jassids or aphids, usually without vein thickening.
