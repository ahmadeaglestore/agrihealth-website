Red Rot of Sugarcane

Upper leaves yellow and dry; split cane is red inside with white cross-bands and a sour smell.

Overview

Red rot is one of the oldest and most damaging diseases of sugarcane in Punjab and Sindh. A fungus grows inside the cane and turns the inner tissue red. Affected canes become light, break easily and give much less juice and sugar.

The disease is carried mostly in infected setts used for planting, and it builds up in ratoon crops. It is worse in warm, humid weather and in waterlogged fields, so in Pakistan it usually becomes clear during and after the monsoon. The surest check is to split a suspected cane along its length.

Symptoms

  • The third or fourth leaf from the top turns yellow-orange, droops and dries; drying then moves down the plant
  • Red spots or streaks on the leaf midrib, often with a pale or ash-coloured centre
  • When a cane is split lengthwise, the inside is red with white patches or bands running across the cane
  • A sour, alcohol-like smell from the split cane
  • Canes become light, shrivelled and break easily
  • In bad cases whole clumps dry up

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Colletotrichum falcatum (Glomerella tucumanensis), carried mainly in infected seed cane (setts).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Planting setts taken from infected canes is the main way it spreads, over short and long distances
  • Rain splash and irrigation or flood water carry spores to nearby plants and fields
  • The fungus survives for some months in soil, crop debris and diseased canes left in the field
  • Ratooning an infected crop carries the disease into the next season
  • Warm, humid weather, waterlogging and growing the same susceptible variety year after year make it worse

What to Do

  • Split a few drying canes lengthwise and look for red tissue with white cross-bands
  • Dig out affected clumps with their roots and burn them, away from the field
  • Make bunds or channels so water from the affected patch does not flow into healthy fields
  • Do not take seed cane from this field
  • Report it to your agriculture officer, who can confirm the disease and advise on a resistant variety for your area

Don't

  • Do not keep an infected crop as a ratoon
  • Do not leave diseased canes and trash lying in the field after harvest

When to call an expert: Call your agriculture officer as soon as you find red canes with white bands, especially if several clumps are drying. Red rot can wipe out a susceptible variety, so the department needs to know about outbreaks.

Prevention

  • Plant a red-rot-resistant variety recommended by your agriculture department
  • Use healthy setts from a disease-free field or a certified seed-cane nursery
  • Throw away any sett that shows reddening at the cut ends or nodes
  • Treat seed cane with an approved fungicide or a heat treatment as advised by your agriculture department
  • Keep the field well drained and avoid waterlogging
  • Rotate the field with other crops for about two years after an attack, and burn crop remains

Problems That Look Similar

  • Sugarcane wilt

    The inside turns dull purple-red to brown without white cross-bands, and canes become hollow and light; there is no sour smell.

  • Top borer damage

    The central shoot dries (dead heart) and you find a caterpillar tunnel, not red tissue with white bands.

  • Whip smut

    A long black whip grows from the top of the cane; the inside is not red.
