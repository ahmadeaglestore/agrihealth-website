Zinc Deficiency

Pale stripes or yellow patches between veins on young leaves; small, bunched leaves; stunted plants.

Overview

Zinc is a micronutrient: plants need only a little of it, but without it growth stops. Unlike nitrogen, zinc does not move easily inside the plant, so symptoms appear on the young and middle leaves rather than the oldest ones. Plants stay short, with small leaves bunched together at the shoot tips.

Zinc shortage is common in alkaline (high-pH) and calcareous soils, which are widespread in Pakistan, and where topsoil has been removed during land levelling. Maize, rice, citrus and wheat are among the crops most often affected. In rice, zinc shortage is known as khaira and has its own guide.

Symptoms

  • Pale yellow or white stripes or patches between the veins of young and middle leaves
  • In maize, a broad white or pale band on each side of the midrib, and new leaves may come out whitish ('white bud')
  • In wheat, pale patches in the middle part of the leaf that turn grey or brown, so the leaf bends and collapses there
  • In citrus and other fruit trees, small, narrow, pointed leaves with yellow between green veins, bunched at the twig tips
  • Short gaps between the nodes, giving a stunted, bunched (rosette) look
  • Delayed maturity and lower yield; in fruit trees, twig dieback and small fruit

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Too little zinc available to the plant, common in calcareous, high-pH soils.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Alkaline (high-pH) and calcareous soils, where zinc is locked up
  • Soils low in organic matter, and sandy soils
  • Topsoil removed during land levelling
  • Heavy use of phosphate fertiliser or very high nitrogen, which can make zinc shortage worse
  • Dry topsoil early in the season, when roots cannot take up zinc

What to Do

  • Compare the leaves: zinc shortage shows on young and middle leaves, nitrogen shortage on the oldest leaves
  • Ask your agriculture officer or a soil testing lab to confirm with a soil or leaf test
  • Apply zinc (for example zinc sulphate) to the soil or as a foliar spray only as advised by your agriculture department for your crop
  • Remember that a foliar spray works fastest for the current crop, while soil application also helps later crops

Don't

  • Don't keep adding more phosphate or nitrogen to 'green up' the crop; it can make zinc shortage worse

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer when young leaves show pale stripes or small, bunched leaves over large areas, or when the symptoms come back every year in the same field.

Prevention

  • Test your soil, especially calcareous soils and land that has been levelled
  • Apply zinc fertiliser at sowing in fields known to be short of zinc, as recommended
  • Add well-rotted farmyard manure or compost
  • Use a balanced fertiliser plan instead of only phosphate and nitrogen
  • In orchards, follow your horticulture department's micronutrient programme

Problems That Look Similar

  • Nitrogen deficiency

    Nitrogen shortage yellows the oldest lower leaves evenly, without stripes between the veins.

  • Citrus greening (HLB)

    Greening gives blotchy, uneven yellow patches that differ on the two halves of the leaf, and lopsided, bitter fruit; zinc shortage gives an even pattern on both sides of the midrib.

  • Iron deficiency

    Iron shortage turns the youngest leaves yellow to almost white with fine green veins, while the leaves stay normal in size.
