Pink Bollworm

Twisted 'rosette' flowers, small holes in green bolls, pink caterpillars inside and stained lint.

Overview

Pink bollworm is a small moth whose caterpillar lives hidden inside cotton flowers and bolls. It eats the developing seeds and cuts and stains the lint, so both yield and fibre quality fall. Because it stays inside the boll, it is hard to see and hard to reach with sprays.

It does most damage in the second half of the cotton season, while flowering and boll setting continue into autumn. It survives the off-season as resting caterpillars in seed, stored seed cotton, ginning factories and leftover cotton sticks with unopened bolls, so clean-up across the whole area after harvest is very important. It can also damage Bt cotton, so other control steps are still needed.

Symptoms

  • Flowers that stay half-closed and twisted (rosette flowers), with a small caterpillar inside
  • Young bolls and squares may drop
  • Small round exit holes in green bolls
  • Black spots on green bolls, sometimes with rotting
  • Small pink caterpillars and damaged seeds inside when you open a green boll
  • Bolls open badly; the lint is stained and of poor quality

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Pectinophora gossypiella (moth caterpillar).

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • Resting caterpillars survive in seed, stored seed cotton, ginning factories and cotton sticks with unopened bolls
  • Crops left standing late into winter let the pest keep breeding
  • Long, staggered sowing in an area gives the moth flowers and bolls for a long time
  • Moths are active at night and lay eggs on flowers, young bolls and tender leaves

What to Do

  • Pick rosette flowers by hand and destroy them, for example by burying them deep away from the field
  • Open a sample of green bolls each week to look for caterpillars and damage
  • Use pheromone traps to watch moth numbers
  • Ask your agriculture officer when to act and which methods (such as pheromone mating disruption) or products are approved

Don't

  • Don't store cotton sticks with unopened bolls near fields or homes into the next season
  • Don't keep a ratoon or extended crop after the main pickings

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer when rosette flowers or damaged bolls appear, or when trap catches rise, so action can be timed before caterpillars enter the bolls.

Prevention

  • Sow at the recommended time, at about the same time as your neighbours
  • Grow approved varieties and end the crop on time
  • Destroy stalks and remaining bolls soon after the last picking
  • Keep ginning factories and seed stores clean, and use traps near them
  • Use pheromone traps or mating-disruption ropes as advised
  • Rotate cotton with other crops

Problems That Look Similar

  • Spotted bollworm

    Brownish spotted caterpillar that also bores into shoot tips, which droop and dry.

  • American bollworm (Helicoverpa)

    Larger green-brown caterpillar that feeds with half its body outside the boll and leaves big holes.
