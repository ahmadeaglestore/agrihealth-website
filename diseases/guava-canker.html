Canker and Scab of Guava

Small rusty-brown raised spots on green guava fruit that turn into corky craters and split the skin.

Overview

Guava canker, also called fruit scab, is a fungal disease that mainly attacks green fruit and only sometimes the leaves. It starts as tiny brown or rust-coloured round spots with raised edges. These become corky, crater-like cankers; the cankers stay shallow, but when several join together the skin cracks open. Badly affected fruit stays small, hard and misshapen, may dry up on the tree and often drops. On leaves it can cause small rusty, angular spots.

The fungus can live inside twigs without causing symptoms and becomes harmful when trees are stressed by drought, waterlogging, wind or pruning damage. It mostly enters fruit through wounds and insect feeding marks, and rain splash spreads its spores. Indian guidance notes that cankerous spots are common on the winter crop, while in the rainy season the disease shows as tiny red specks. Some varieties suffer more than others.

Symptoms

  • Tiny brown or rust-coloured round spots on green fruit
  • Spots get raised edges and turn corky and crater-like
  • Several cankers join together and the fruit skin cracks or splits
  • Fruit stays small, hard and misshapen, may dry up on the tree and drop
  • Small rusty, angular spots on some leaves

Causes & Spread

Main cause: Pestalotiopsis psidii (also called Pestalotia psidii) and related fungi.

How it spreads and what makes it worse

  • The fungus enters fruit through wounds and insect feeding marks
  • Rain splash spreads spores from infected fruit and twigs
  • Trees stressed by drought, waterlogging, wind or pruning damage are more affected
  • Crowded canopies keep fruit wet and make wounding more likely

What to Do

  • Pick off fruit with cankers and collect fallen fruit; destroy them away from the orchard
  • Prune out dead twigs and crowded, overlapping branches
  • Avoid wounding fruit during work in the orchard
  • Ask your agriculture officer about controlling insects that feed on the fruit, and whether a fungicide spray is needed and which product is approved

Don't

  • Don't leave diseased or dried fruit hanging on the trees

When to call an expert: Contact your agriculture officer if many green fruits have rusty corky spots or are cracking and dropping.

Prevention

  • Space trees properly and prune every year so the canopy stays open
  • Water regularly, avoid waterlogging and give balanced fertiliser so trees are not stressed
  • Plant windbreaks around exposed orchards
  • Plant healthy nursery plants
  • Check green fruit regularly, especially after rain

Problems That Look Similar

  • Guava anthracnose

    Sunken, dark, soft spots that rot the flesh, with pink or orange spore masses in damp weather; not raised and corky.

  • Algal leaf spot (red rust)

    Round spots on leaves with rusty-orange velvety tufts in the centre, caused by an alga; mostly on leaves, not cankers on fruit.
